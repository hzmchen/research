# RASt 06: what "peak veh/h" means and where the number comes from

> 🤖 Generated by a coding agent — not human-verified.

**Executive summary.** All RASt 06 traffic-volume figures (the five
cross-section bands, the mixed-traffic thresholds, the crossing-facility and
bus-stop limits) are **peak-hour volumes of motorised traffic in vehicles per
hour (Kfz/h)**, by default **referred to the cross-section** — both directions,
all lanes summed — and taken as **either today's measured level or the forecast
level, "depending on the design task"**. That one clause is the entire
definition: RASt 06 has **no glossary entry for "peak hour"** and prescribes
**no measurement or derivation method**. The operationalisation lives outside
the guideline: in German practice the design-hour convention is the
**n-th-highest hour of the year** from the HBS/SVZ world (since HBS 2015 the
**50th-highest hour**, called *maßgebende stündliche Verkehrsstärke*, MSV, or
*Bemessungsverkehrsstärke* q_B; before that the 30th), while municipal practice
for city streets typically uses **short-duration counts** or the rule of thumb
**peak hour ≈ 10 % of DTV** — which BASt research grades "sehr ungenau".

> **Key insight:** RASt 06 deliberately treats the peak-hour volume as an
> *input*, not something it defines. It is a street-*design* guideline: it
> tells you which cross-section fits a given Kfz/h band, and leaves measuring
> or forecasting that Kfz/h to the survey/forecast rule set (HBS, SVZ,
> Verkehrsprognose). Anyone quoting "RASt 06 requires X at N Kfz/h" must
> therefore separately state *which* peak hour N is — measured or forecast,
> which year, which hour convention — because RASt 06 itself does not say.

Quotes below are from the official English translation (FGSV 200 E,
"Directives for the Design of Urban Roads", translation 2012 of the 2006
edition) retrieved as publisher PDF; validation tags and source grades as in
the [topic README](README.md).

## 1. What RASt 06 actually says

The load-bearing passage is **section 5.1.1, step 3** (printed p. 33) of the
four-step procedure for finding a recommended cross-section [V]:

> "Step 3. Defining the volumes of motorised traffic, **grouped into five
> partly overlapping peak-hour ranges, which may refer to current or forecast
> levels, depending on the design task, referred to the cross-section**:
> – < 400 vehicles per hour
> – 400 – 1,000 vehicles per hour
> – 800 – 1,800 vehicles per hour
> – 1,600 – 2,600 vehicles per hour
> – > 2,600 vehicles per hour."

Three further qualifications in the same section [V]:

- Downward compatibility: *"A recommended cross-section for a specific volume
  of traffic is usually also suitable for lower volumes of motor vehicle
  traffic from a traffic engineering point of view."*
- The overlaps are deliberate decision zones, resolved by crossing ease:
  *"Traffic volumes especially in the range from 400 to 1,000 vehicles per
  hour – result in differences related to the ease of crossing a road, which
  need to be considered when selecting a profile."*
- The carriageway capacity figures of section 6.1.1 (the 1,400–2,200 /
  1,800–2,600 veh/h values used in [doc 01 §3.1](01-berlin-vierstreifige-verkehrsfuehrung.md))
  are *"intended as a rough guide for potential application, and do not take
  into account the limiting capacity of junctions"*; footnote 36 defers the
  real capacity methodology to *"the next update to the HBS"*.

A full-text search of the translation confirms **"peak hour" is never
defined** — no glossary, no terms section; the word appears only in section
5.1.1, in two tables and one figure (see §4 below), and in vertical-alignment
contexts ("peak crest") unrelated to traffic volume.

## 2. So what does "peak" refer to?

Putting the pieces together:

| Aspect | Reading | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Quantity | Motor vehicles only (Kfz/h) — pedestrians and cyclists have their *own* peak-hour tables (see §4) | [V] 5.1.1, table 27 |
| Time slice | The **peak hour** (German *Spitzenstunde*) — the highest-volume single hour of a normal day; *which* hour/day is not specified | [V] 5.1.1; no definition found |
| Spatial reference | **Cross-section** (both directions + all lanes summed) by default; explicit per-direction exceptions are flagged where they apply | [V] 5.1.1 ("referred to the cross-section") |
| Temporal reference | **Current or forecast** — *"may refer to current or forecast levels, depending on the design task"*: redesign of an existing street → measured values; new build / capacity expansion → forecast values for the design horizon | [V] 5.1.1 |
| Heavy-traffic share | Not part of the band definition, but shifts thresholds and capacities (*"depending on the … traffic mix"*; mixed cycle/car operation quoted *"with 6 % heavy traffic"*) | [V] 6.1.1, 6.1.7.2 |

The per-direction exceptions worth knowing [V]:

- **Crossing facilities** (figure 77): the selection chart *"relates only to
  the peak hour for the direction carrying the heavier load"*, with indicative
  limits *"750 veh/h peak hour in cross-section"* and *"100 pedestrians in the
  peak hour"* at 50 km/h; *"With a central median the higher peak hour flow in
  the Nomogram is used."*
- **Bus stop forms** (table 41): stops on the carriageway / kerb build-outs
  up to *"750 vehicles per hour in each direction"* (build-outs unproblematic
  to ~650 veh/h per direction at dense headways), with a footnote pointing to
  the HBS.
- **Carriageway capacities** (6.1.1): two-lane figures are *"total for both
  directions"*, four-lane and divided cross-sections *"in each direction"* —
  mixing these up doubles/halves the apparent headroom (already tabulated in
  [doc 01 §3.1](01-berlin-vierstreifige-verkehrsfuehrung.md)).

## 3. Where the Kfz/h number comes from in practice

RASt 06's silence is filled by three routes, in descending order of rigour:

### 3.1 Measured: the n-th-highest hour of the year (HBS/SVZ convention)

The formal German design-hour convention is defined in the HBS world, not in
RASt. The Bavarian road administration states the general definition [V,
BAYSIS]: *"Als Bemessungsverkehrsstärke wird allgemein die Verkehrsstärke der
n-ten Stunde eines Jahres definiert"* — sort all 8,760 hourly volumes of a
year descending; the n-th value is the design volume.

Which n: the federal introduction decree for HBS 2015 (ARS 14/2015, BMVI,
26 Aug 2015) fixes it [V]:

> "Für die verkehrstechnische Bemessung bitte ich, künftig in der Regel die
> **Verkehrsstärke in der 50. höchstbelasteten Stunde des Jahres** als
> maßgebende stündliche Verkehrsstärke MSV (bzw. Bemessungsverkehrsstärke
> q_B) zugrunde zu legen. Diese wird im Rahmen der Straßenverkehrszählung SVZ
> bzw. der Jahresauswertung der automatischen Dauerzählstellen regelmäßig von
> der Bundesanstalt für Straßenwesen … ermittelt und veröffentlicht."

Caveats on scope: the decree *introduces* this for Bundesfernstraßen in
federal Baulast and only *recommends* it for other road authorities [V]; HBS
2001 had used the **30th** hour, and BASt's own research found *"hinsichtlich
der Wahl einer n-ten Stunde ergeben sich nur geringe Unterschiede zwischen
der 30. und 50. Stunde"* [V, BASt Foko 02/2018]. The same decree sets the
acceptance criterion the peak-hour volume is tested against: at least
**QSV D** (level-of-service grade D) for new/upgraded schemes [V].

For city streets, year-long duration curves exist only where permanent
detectors run; municipal practice substitutes **short-duration counts**
(typically 12-h weekday counts, Tue/Thu) from which the peak hour is read
directly — Berlin's survey methodology is exactly this (see §3.4 below and
the companion [DTV/DTVw reference](../berlin-traffic-groundwork/dtv-dtvw-reference.md)).

### 3.2 Forecast: the Verkehrsprognose for the design horizon

For new construction or capacity decisions, the peak hour is taken from a
traffic model/forecast — ARS 14/2015 [V]: *"Der verkehrstechnischen Bemessung
bitte ich grundsätzlich aktuelle Verkehrsprognosen … zugrunde zu legen"*
(trend extrapolation only *"im Ausnahmefall"*). Models usually output **daily**
workday volumes (DTVw), so the forecast peak hour is itself derived via
day-curve shares — which is route 3.3. The TVO worked example in
[doc 01 §3.2](01-berlin-vierstreifige-verkehrsfuehrung.md) shows the pattern:
the published justification quotes 22,000–33,000 Kfz/workday for 2030 and the
four-lane conclusion presupposes the peak-hour conversion.

### 3.3 Inferred from daily volume: the ~10 % rule of thumb

Where only DTV/DTVw is available, the peak hour is estimated as a share of
the day. Reference points:

- Wikipedia's *Verkehrsstärke* article (grade D, fetched): from the standard
  15–19 h short count *"wird etwa ein Drittel der durchschnittlichen täglichen
  Verkehrsstärke (DTV in Kfz/24 h) und die dreifache Menge der MSV
  (Kfz/Spitzenstunde) ermittelt. **DTV ist also etwa 10-mal so groß wie
  MSV.**"* [V — i.e. peak hour ≈ 10 % of DTV]
- Consultant practice (Verkehrsgutachten) cites HBS day-curve tables with
  peak-hour shares around **10–11.5 % of DTV** for the cross-section [S,
  grade C — e.g. a Strausberg study citing "Tabelle 2-8 des HBS … 11,5 % an
  DTV"; not independently re-fetched].
- BASt's research verdict on the shortcut [V, Foko 02/2018]: *"Das früher
  gängige Verfahren einer überschlägigen Berechnung, die
  Bemessungsverkehrsstärke als Prozentwert des durchschnittlichen täglichen
  Verkehrs (DTV) zu ermitteln, **erwies sich als sehr ungenau**."*

This 10 % heuristic is what links RASt 06's top bands to daily volumes — the
"1,600–2,600 Kfz/h ≈ 20,000–25,000 Kfz/day" inference already flagged in
doc 01 as *not* a codified threshold.

### 3.4 Berlin specifics — and a trap

Berlin's official conversion paper (SenUMVK, *"Hinweise und Faktoren zur
Umrechnung von Verkehrsmengen"*, April 2022, grade A, fetched) confirms the
framing: Berlin's maps and model deliver **DTVw**, weekend volumes sit below
workdays, and *"Bemessungsfragen sich an standardisierten Spitzenstunden
orientieren"* [V] — i.e. design questions run on standardised peak hours that
must be obtained from counts/detectors, not from the published daily map
value. It supplies DTVw→DTV factors (Kfz 0.91, Lkw 0.82, Stand 10/2021) [V]
but **no DTV→peak-hour factor**.

The trap: the same paper's *"Maßgebende Verkehrsstärke M"* table (M_t ≈
0.055 × DTV daytime, 0.015 × DTV night) looks like a peak-hour factor but is
the **RLS-19 noise-assessment hourly mean** over 6–22 h / 22–6 h — an
*average* hour, roughly half the peak hour. Despite sharing the word
"maßgebend", it is not the HBS MSV and must not be used as a RASt 06 input
[V — same document; cross-checked against the RLS-19 context it cites].

| Table 41 bus stop forms | ≤ 750 veh/h | **per direction** |
| Table 27 shared ped/cycle paths | 70/100/150 (P+C)/h, cyclists ≤ ⅓ | pedestrians + cyclists, peak hour |

## 5. Caveats & open questions

- **German original wording not verbatim-verified.** All RASt quotes are from
  the official English translation (FGSV 200 E); the German terms used here
  (*Spitzenstunde*, *Belastungsbereiche*) are reconstructed from secondary
  German sources [S]. Before citing in formal contexts, check the German
  edition's section 5.1.1 wording (expected: *"Kfz/h in der Spitzenstunde"*,
  *"vorhandene oder prognostizierte"*, *"bezogen auf den Querschnitt"*).
- **HBS 2015 urban-streets part (Teil S) not retrieved** (paywalled); whether
  it states an urban-specific design-hour convention or day-curve table
  (the "Tabelle 2-8" cited in Gutachten) is unverified.
- **Applicability of the 50th-hour rule to municipal streets** is a
  recommendation, not an obligation (ARS 14/2015 binds Bundesfernstraßen);
  what Berlin formally applies for city-street Bemessung was not found in a
  published source — likely case-by-case in Gutachten.
- The five bands' figures should be re-verified against the **forthcoming
  RASt revision** (in progress per FGSV 26502, cf. doc 01 §8).

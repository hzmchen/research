# Four-lane roads in Berlin: when is a "vierstreifige Verkehrsführung" necessary or allowed?

> 🤖 Generated by a coding agent — not human-verified.

**Research date:** 2026-06-10 · **Status:** v1
**Question:** Under Berlin (and applicable federal German) road-planning regulations, when is a four-lane traffic layout — *vierstreifige Verkehrsführung* — required, and when is it permitted?

**Executive summary.** No German statute mandates four lanes at a given
traffic volume. In *planning*, the lane count is an output of the process:
network function class (RIN 2008 → Berlin's übergeordnetes Straßennetz) plus
capacity design (RASt 06/HBS) produce a cross-section proposal, which must
then pass planning approval (§ 22 BerlStrG / § 17 FStrG), environmental
assessment (mandatory for new 4+-lane federal roads ≥ 5 km), noise/air rules,
and the Berlin Mobility Act's modal priorities. Only Bundesautobahnen are
four-lane by legal definition (§ 1 Abs. 3 FStrG). In *operations*, nothing
forces keeping four car lanes — § 45 StVO orders can reallocate them, and
since the 2020 pop-up-bike-lane rulings the bar is low. In *work zones*, a
temporary 4+0 routing is permitted whenever RSA 21 geometry and width rules
can be met.

> **Key insight:** "vierstreifig" is never an entitlement and rarely an
> obligation — it is the *result* of a forecast-driven design process that
> must survive several legal gates, and the same legal system makes it much
> easier to take a car lane away (a traffic order suffices) than to add one
> (full planning approval with EIA).

## 1. Terminology: three distinct regulatory senses

German road regulation counts *Fahrstreifen* (lanes); the technically correct
term is **vierstreifig** (four lanes in cross-section, usually 2+2), while
colloquial usage says "vierspurig". *Verkehrsführung* ("traffic routing") is
used in three different regulatory contexts, each with its own rules:

| Sense | Meaning | Governing regime |
|-------|---------|------------------|
| **A — Planning** | Permanent four-lane cross-section of a road | FStrG / BerlStrG (Planfeststellung), UVPG, FGSV design rules (RIN, RASt 06, RAA), MobG BE |
| **B — Operations** | How existing pavement is allocated to lanes (e.g. keeping or removing a 4-lane car layout, bus lanes, bike lanes) | § 45 StVO, traffic authority orders |
| **C — Work zones** | Temporary routing during roadworks, e.g. the **4+0-Verkehrsführung** (both directions squeezed onto one carriageway) | RSA 21 work-zone guidelines |

There is **no statute that mandates "four lanes" as such** in sense A. The
obligation or permission emerges from the interplay of (i) functional network
classification, (ii) capacity/design guidelines, and (iii) the legal gates a
project must pass (planning approval, environmental assessment, noise/air
rules, and in Berlin the Mobility Act's modal priorities). Sections 3–4 unpack
this; sections 5–6 cover senses B and C.

## 2. The regulatory stack for Berlin road planning

1. **Federal statutes**
   - **FStrG** (Bundesfernstraßengesetz) for federal roads crossing Berlin
     (A 100, A 111, A 113, A 115, B 1/B 2/B 5/B 96a …). § 1 Abs. 3 [V]:
     *"Bundesautobahnen sind Bundesfernstraßen, die nur für den Schnellverkehr
     mit Kraftfahrzeugen bestimmt und so angelegt sind, dass sie frei von
     höhengleichen Kreuzungen und für Zu- und Abfahrt mit besonderen
     Anschlussstellen ausgestattet sind. Sie sollen getrennte Fahrbahnen für
     den Richtungsverkehr haben."* — separated directional carriageways make
     an Autobahn structurally **at least four-lane (2+2)** by definition.
   - **UVPG** (environmental impact assessment) — thresholds in § 3.2 below.
   - **BauGB** — roads can also be fixed in a Bebauungsplan
     (§ 9 Abs. 1 Nr. 11 BauGB, Verkehrsflächen).
2. **Berlin statutes**
   - **Berliner Straßengesetz (BerlStrG)**. § 20 classifies public roads by
     traffic significance [V, quoted in SenMVKU 2025]: *Straßen I. Ordnung*
     (large-scale/long-distance & regional traffic, together with federal
     roads), *Straßen II. Ordnung* (inter-district traffic, connection of
     districts to I. Ordnung), and *sonstige öffentliche Straßen*. § 22
     requires **Planfeststellung** (formal planning approval) before building
     or substantially altering Straßen I. Ordnung, and before building Straßen
     II. Ordnung [S — Haufe text of § 22]. § 27 authorizes the
     **Landesstraßenplan** (legal ordinance of 15 Aug 2007, GVBl. 24/07) which
     fixes the I./II. Ordnung network and *"ist die Grundlage für die
     Entscheidung, ob die auszubauende oder neu zu bauende Straße der
     Planfeststellungspflicht unterliegt"* [V, SenMVKU 2025].
   - **Berliner Mobilitätsgesetz (MobG BE, 2018)** — Germany's first mobility
     act. § 5 Abs. 1 [S, statute mirror]: the share of the *Umweltverbund*
     (walking, cycling, public transport) is to be increased; § 26 Abs. 5
     gives public transport *"Vorrang vor dem motorisierten
     Individualverkehr"* within existing law; § 43 requires cycling
     infrastructure along **all Hauptverkehrsstraßen** [S — ADFC/Changing
     Cities monitoring]. This does not ban four-lane layouts, but every
     cross-section must now also fit mandatory bike infrastructure, which in
     constrained rights-of-way works against 2+2 car lanes.
3. **Planning instruments**
   - **Übergeordnetes Straßennetz ("StEP-Netz")**, published with the
     Stadtentwicklungsplan Mobilität und Verkehr (**StEP MoVe**). Berlin's
     network is ~5,350 km, of which ~1,500 km is übergeordnet [V]. It is
     classified per **RIN 2008** into *Verbindungsfunktionsstufen* [V, SenMVKU
     2025]:

     | Stufe | Berlin name | Examples |
     |-------|-------------|----------|
     | 0 | kontinentale Straßenverbindung | few km of A 10 at Karow/Buch |
     | I | großräumige Straßenverbindung | all Stadtautobahnen; parts of B 1, B 2, B 5, B 96a |
     | II | übergeordnete Straßenverbindung | Landsberger Allee, Tempelhofer Damm |
     | III | örtliche Straßenverbindung | Groß-Ziethener Straße, Königsheideweg |
     | IV | Ergänzungsstraßen | Wilhelminenhofstraße, Montardstraße |

   - The classification is consequential: *"die Einstufung der einzelnen
     Straßen gibt im gesamten Regelwerk für die Straße maßgebend, wenn auch
     nicht allein, die Ableitung von Gestaltungsmerkmalen (Querschnitt, …),
     Geschwindigkeit, Verkehrsrechtlichen Anordnungen … vor"* [V, SenMVKU
     2025].
4. **Technical design rules (FGSV)** — not statutes, but "generally accepted
   rules of technology" that bind via introduction decrees and via the
   weighing duty in planning approval: **RIN 2008** (network functions),
   **RASt 06** (urban streets, design speed ≤ 70 km/h), **RAA** (motorways,
   incl. city motorways — all standard cross-sections are ≥ 2+2, consistent
   with § 1 Abs. 3 FStrG), **RAL 2012** (rural roads; only the top design
   class is four-lane — not separately verified), **HBS** (capacity manual,
   level-of-service grades A–F).

## 3. Sense A — when is a permanent four-lane cross-section *necessary*?

### 3.1 Function + capacity, not a statutory lane count

Necessity is established case-by-case in the planning process, by combining:

- **Network function**: a road classified Stufe I/II (or Straße I. Ordnung)
  is *designed* for large-scale traffic; the RIN-based classification feeds
  the cross-section choice via RASt 06/RAA [V, SenMVKU 2025].
- **Capacity analysis**: the forecast traffic volume (Verkehrsprognose) is
  checked against cross-section capacity per HBS. RASt 06 treats two-lane
  urban cross-sections as workable over a wide range — its design tables span
  < 400 to > 2,600 Kfz/h with carriageway widths of 5.50–7.00 m [V, secondary
  source]; with bus service at ≥ 10 min headways it cites ~750 Kfz/h per
  direction as a guidance value for two-lane Hauptverkehrsstraßen [S, VCD].
  Four lanes become the design answer when the forecast load can no longer be
  handled at acceptable quality (HBS level of service) by two lanes plus
  junction improvements. A frequently used practitioner heuristic is that
  urban arterials above roughly **18,000–25,000 vehicles/day (DTV)** are
  four-lane candidates — *heuristic, not codified; not verified against the
  RASt 06 text, which is paywalled (FGSV)*. (On what DTV/DTVw mean and where
  Berlin publishes them, see the companion topic's
  [DTV & DTVw reference](../berlin-traffic-groundwork/dtv-dtvw-reference.md).)
- **Autobahn status**: anything planned as Bundesautobahn is at least 2+2 by
  legal definition (§ 1 Abs. 3 FStrG [V]) and RAA standard cross-sections.

### 3.2 Berlin worked example: Tangentiale Verbindung Ost (TVO)

The TVO — a planned ~7.2 km *Straßenverbindung 1. Ordnung* from
Spindlersfelder Straße/An der Wuhlheide to Märkische Allee (B 158)/Alt
Friedrichsfelde (B 1/B 5) through Marzahn-Hellersdorf, Treptow-Köpenick and
Lichtenberg — is the current canonical case [S, berlin.de / uvp-verbund.de]:

- Forecast demand for 2030: **22,000–33,000 vehicles per workday** in
  cross-section; per the Senate's planning documents these volumes
  *"indicate the need for a four-lane cross-section"* [S, berlin.de].
- Environmental groups and some district politicians demanded a two-lane
  variant; the variant assessment weighed traffic effectiveness, costs,
  noise and air pollution [S].
- The plan was publicly displayed for Planfeststellung in May 2024
  (Amtsblatt für Berlin Nr. 18, 3 May 2024) with a full environmental impact
  assessment [S, uvp-verbund.de].

So in Berlin practice, "necessary" means: **the traffic forecast, run through
the FGSV capacity framework, justifies four lanes, and that justification
survives the variant comparison and weighing (Abwägung) in the planning
approval**. A recently completed counterpart is the A 100 16. Bauabschnitt
(Neukölln → Treptow), opened to traffic per the Senate's 10/2025 network
update [V, SenMVKU 2025].

## 4. Sense A — when is a four-lane cross-section *allowed*? (legal gates)

1. **Planning approval (Planfeststellung).** New construction or substantial
   alteration of Straßen I. Ordnung — which four-lane arterials in practice
   are — requires Planfeststellung under § 22 BerlStrG (federal roads:
   § 17 FStrG). The Landesstraßenplan determines whether the obligation
   applies [V, SenMVKU 2025]. The approval requires *Planrechtfertigung*
   (the project must be reasonably required, typically shown via the traffic
   forecast) and a defensible *Abwägung* of all affected interests.
2. **Environmental impact assessment (UVP).** Anlage 1 UVPG for federal
   roads [V, gesetze-im-internet.de]:
   - Nr. 14.3: any Bundesautobahn or Bundesstraße that is a *Schnellstraße*
     (per the European Agreement on Main International Traffic Arteries) —
     **UVP mandatory (X)**.
   - Nr. 14.4: *"Bau einer neuen vier- oder mehrstreifigen Bundesstraße, wenn
     diese neue Straße eine durchgehende Länge von 5 km oder mehr aufweist"*
     — **UVP mandatory (X)**. The four-lane threshold is thus itself a
     regulatory trigger.
   - Nr. 14.5: four-or-more-lane rebuild/realignment of an existing
     Bundesstraße ≥ 10 km — **UVP mandatory (X)**.
   - Nr. 14.6: other Bundesstraße construction — general screening
     (*allgemeine Vorprüfung*, A).
   - For Berlin's own Straßen I./II. Ordnung, Land EIA law applies in
     conjunction with the UVPG; in practice large projects (TVO) run a full
     UVP inside the Planfeststellung [S].
3. **Noise and air quality.** New construction or substantial alteration
   triggers the noise limits of the 16. BImSchV (Verkehrslärmschutzverordnung);
   air-quality limits (39. BImSchV) constrain the Abwägung. A four-lane
   project that cannot meet these without disproportionate mitigation is not
   approvable. *(Standard doctrine; statutes not re-fetched for this note.)*
4. **Berlin Mobility Act and StEP MoVe.** MobG BE does not prohibit road
   expansion, but its Umweltverbund priorities (§ 5, § 26 Abs. 5 [S]) and the
   § 43 duty to provide cycling infrastructure on all Hauptverkehrsstraßen
   [S] are material in the Abwägung and in cross-section design: a new
   four-lane road must *also* carry compliant bike and pedestrian
   infrastructure, raising its land take and cost. Current Berlin planning
   policy (StEP MoVe) favors reallocation over expansion inside the S-Bahn
   ring; expansion projects concentrate on tangential/orbital links (TVO,
   A 100) [S].

**Bottom line (sense A):** a four-lane layout is *allowed* if and only if a
planning-approval decision (or a qualifying Bebauungsplan) carries it through
justification, EIA, noise/air limits, and weighing — and it is *necessary*
only where the forecast function and volume of the corridor exceed what a
two-lane cross-section can serve at acceptable HBS quality. Lane count is an
output of this process, never a free-standing entitlement.

## 5. Sense B — operational four-lane layouts and their removal (§ 45 StVO)

Whether an existing four-lane road *stays* four lanes for cars is an
operational question decided by the road traffic authority (in Berlin:
SenMVKU Abteilung VI Verkehrsmanagement for the übergeordnetes Netz Stufe
I–IV [V, SenMVKU 2025]) via orders under **§ 45 StVO**:

- § 45 Abs. 9 requires traffic signs/installations only *"wo dies auf Grund
  der besonderen Umstände zwingend erforderlich ist"*, and restrictions of
  flowing traffic in principle only where local conditions create a danger
  significantly exceeding the general risk ("qualifizierte Gefahrenlage")
  [V, paraphrase].
- **Pop-up bike lane litigation (2020):** Berlin converted car lanes — on
  several four-lane arterials — into temporary bike lanes during the
  pandemic. The VG Berlin (04.09.2020) initially held such conversions
  require a concrete qualified danger; the **OVG Berlin-Brandenburg
  (06.10.2020, OVG 1 S 115/20 / 1 S 116/20)** reversed in summary
  proceedings: traffic counts and accident data sufficed to justify
  separating bike from car traffic, so the lanes stayed [S, LTO/ESV/vhw].
- The current § 45 Abs. 9 text exempts several categories (including cycling
  infrastructure, Tempo-30 zones, bus lanes) from the heightened
  qualified-danger threshold [V, paraphrase of fetched text], lowering the
  bar for reallocating one of four lanes.

So in operations the asymmetry is the reverse of planning: nothing forces the
authority to *keep* four car lanes; reallocation to bus/bike lanes is lawful
under § 45 StVO without any Planfeststellung, since the road body itself is
unchanged.

## 6. Sense C — temporary "4+0-Verkehrsführung" in work zones (RSA 21)

On separated-carriageway roads (Autobahnen, city motorways), the **4+0
Verkehrsführung** routes all four lanes (2+2) onto a single carriageway so
the other can be rebuilt without traffic [S, Wikipedia "4+0-System"].
Governed by the **RSA 21** (Richtlinien für die Sicherung von Arbeitsstellen
an Straßen; successor to RSA 95):

- **Allowed** only where the remaining carriageway's width and pavement
  strength suffice [S]; transitions must be developed 100–150 m ahead of the
  crossover for 3+1/4+0/5+0 layouts [S].
- **Minimum lane widths** (via stvo2go summary of RSA 21, grade C source
  [V]): urban streets normally ≥ 3.00 m per lane (2.85 m on short stretches
  below 50 km/h); on Autobahnen in 4+0 operation ~3.25 m for the main lane
  and 2.60 m for the narrow lane in work zones up to 6 km, with wider
  requirements for longer work zones.
- In Berlin, work-zone traffic orders on roads of Stufe I–III are handled by
  SenMVKU Abteilung VI; Stufe-IV and lower roads by the districts [V,
  SenMVKU 2025].

Here "vierstreifig" is *permitted* as a temporary state whenever the geometry
and safety rules of RSA 21 can be met — and it is *necessary* in the sense
that maintaining four running lanes during works on heavily loaded motorways
is the standard way to avoid capacity collapse.

## 7. Answer in brief

| Question | Answer |
|----------|--------|
| Is there a law mandating four lanes at volume X? | No. Lane count results from RIN function class + HBS/RASt 06 capacity design, confirmed in Planfeststellung. |
| When is 2+2 structurally required? | Bundesautobahnen: separated directional carriageways per § 1 Abs. 3 FStrG → at least four lanes. |
| When does practice deem four lanes necessary? | Forecast volumes beyond two-lane quality limits (TVO: 22,000–33,000 Kfz/workday → four-lane cross-section). |
| What does a four-lane project legally require in Berlin? | Planfeststellung (§ 22 BerlStrG / § 17 FStrG) incl. justification and Abwägung; UVP (mandatory for new 4+ lane Bundesstraßen ≥ 5 km, Nr. 14.4 Anlage 1 UVPG); 16./39. BImSchV compliance; MobG BE-compliant bike/pedestrian provision. |
| Can four car lanes be reduced without re-planning? | Yes — § 45 StVO orders (bus/bike lanes); since the pop-up-bike-lane rulings and the § 45 Abs. 9 exemptions, the legal bar is low. |
| Temporary four-lane routing in work zones? | 4+0/2+2 per RSA 21, if width/pavement suffice; minimum lane widths ~3.25 m/2.60 m on motorways, ≥ 3.00 m urban. |
